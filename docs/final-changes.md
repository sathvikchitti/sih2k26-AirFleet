## Phase 3: fleet readiness KPIs

- Added `mission_capable_rate_pct`, `readiness_target_pct`, `gap_to_target_pct`, `nmcs_count`, and `aircraft_down_for_maintenance` at fleet and base level. Existing availability fields and health-only classifications remain unchanged. The mission-capable rate is the percentage of aircraft currently classified AVAILABLE; the target defaults to 75% and is configurable through `READINESS_TARGET_PCT` (finite value from 0 through 100). The gap is the nonnegative percentage-point shortfall.
- `nmcs_count` is a distinct-aircraft resource-shortage proxy: it counts non-available aircraft with an open work order whose known component cannot be serviced from local, unreserved inventory above the catalog reserve and condition threshold. Pending outbound transfers and existing reservations are treated as commitments; inbound transfers are not counted as received stock. Unknown components are excluded rather than inferred. `aircraft_down_for_maintenance` counts MAINTENANCE and WAITING_FOR_PART aircraft separately from risk-only states. These snapshot metrics do not treat FD001 cycles as hours or claim operational certification.

## Phase 4: indicative cycle-based spares demand forecast

- Added read-only `GET /api/resources/forecast?horizon_cycles=30`. It predicts fractional expected demand from finite cycle RUL point/std/envelope values with the normal CDF approximation `P(RUL <= horizon)`. Zero standard deviation uses a deterministic threshold. Predictions whose supplied envelope does not overlap `[0, horizon]`, mock flight-hour predictions, and missing/invalid interval values are not included; the response distinguishes `no_eligible_predictions` (expected demand null) from eligible predictions with actual zero expected demand.
- The endpoint adds per-base/engine-component on-hand, reserved/minimum reserve, condition, transfer-available stock, inventory after existing work-order/pending-stock claims, inbound categories, expected demand, integer projected shortfall, and whole-unit suggested moves. `projected_shortfall = max(0, ceil(expected_demand) - committed_available_for_transfer - eligible_unbound_inbound)`; expected demand itself remains fractional. Only unbound inbound that remains serviceable above reserves can offset it. WO- or aircraft-linked inbound and planner-proposed WO moves are shown separately and remain earmarked for those existing needs. Open WOs for the same aircraft/component are excluded from future probabilistic demand.
- Donor selection reuses the shared `planning_service.allocation_preview` claim ledger and `resource_service.search_from_snapshot` hard constraints. Reported post-commitment balances are frozen; a separate shared cloned ledger debits sequential suggestions so no donor unit is proposed twice. Suggestions are deterministic in base/component order, not a global optimizer. The GET performs no inventory, transfer, work-order, or audit writes. Suggestions remain proposals; existing transfer-creation authorization and live inventory checks still apply.
- The cloned donor-suggestion ledger also holds up to `ceil(expected_demand)` units at each base/component, limited to currently serviceable uncommitted units. These `forecast_protected_units` reduce donor proposals that would leave the source below its own forecast need; they are preview-only and do not change the reported physical or committed inventory balances.
- FD001's RUL-only model does not identify components. The response labels the configurable engine-to-component map `assumed mapping for demo`; the default maps odd-numbered example engines to HP-204 and even-numbered engines to OE-310. `FD001_ENGINE_PART_MAPPING` accepts JSON engine-ID to engine-category catalog part-number values; invalid or non-engine parts are rejected. Normal-CDF demand is indicative and uncalibrated, not `failure_probability`, a subsystem diagnosis, or an airworthiness decision.
- With the Task 2 mapping and the synthetic FD001 operations fixture at the UI's default 60-cycle horizon, a read-only probe found 2 eligible predictions, 7 outside the horizon, and one feasible HP-204 proposal from Charlie to Bravo (one unit, 260 km, estimated 4.3 hours). Charlie had one transferable HP-204 unit before the cloned preview ledger debited the proposal and zero afterward; no inventory was changed by the forecast call. The forecast proposal is not a transfer: a Logistics user must submit a separate `POST /api/transfers`, which creates a pending request and rechecks permission and live inventory.

## Phase 5: paired fleet-scale allocation simulation

- Added a frozen synthetic resource context and threaded it through the existing feasibility search, ranking, shared allocation ledger, and readiness simulator. Default live calls retain their existing resource data and policy behavior. The evaluation uses 300 deterministic paired scenarios (seed `20261018`), each with 40 aircraft, 6 bases, 8 catalog parts, and 12 concurrent work orders. Six parts receive two simultaneous requests each; the other two are catalog-only with no demand.
- Compared the existing production ranking, nearest-feasible FCFS, seeded random-feasible, and local-stock/procurement-only policies. All use identical frozen scenario inputs and the same minimum-reserve/condition feasibility rules. The first three share a fixed 24-hour procurement fallback only after no feasible local or remote source exists; local-only never allocates remotely. The report includes per-policy component outcomes, paired policy deltas, and 95% scenario-bootstrap intervals. There is no selected winner.
- Results: production ranking mean time-weighted availability 84.2726%, mean/median censored aircraft-hours lost 301.9657/298.5500, and unserved work-order rate 4.5556%. Nearest-FCFS was 84.2709%, 301.9983/299.6500 hours, and 4.5278%; its paired 95% intervals versus production include zero for all three headline differences, so these scenarios do not show a clear distinction between those policies. Random-feasible averaged 84.0648% availability and 3.9903 more censored aircraft-hours lost; local-only/procurement averaged 79.2229% and 96.9543 more lost hours. These are simulated outcomes under the stated fixed assumptions, not observed fleet performance.
- The input scarcity diagnostic sums system-wide units passing the same inventory quantity-minus-reserved-minus-minimum-reserve and catalog minimum-condition checks before allocation. Under paired demand, some-but-insufficient supply occurred in 31–45 of 300 scenarios per demanded component; no serviceable system-wide stock occurred in 14–31. Those totals do not guarantee that stock is reachable from every target base. Per-policy per-component completed/unserved outcomes are in the report; SIM-007 and SIM-008 have no generated demand.
- Recovery treats synthetic repairs as restoring affected aircraft under a fixed LOW-risk simulation prediction. Multiple work orders on one aircraft count that aircraft once, using its latest completion; unfinished work is censored at the 48-hour horizon. This does not establish FD001 health recovery or translate cycle-based RUL into hours. Component demand and all outcomes are synthetic.
